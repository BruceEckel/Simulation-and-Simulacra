# Mesmerize

Something to watch, not something to play.

A few thousand points of light drift on a slow, invisible current. They gather into strands,
wind around quiet centres, come apart, and gather again somewhere else. The pattern never
repeats and never finishes. You can leave it running in the corner of a screen, or sit with it
for a while, whichever you feel like.

There is nothing to win, nothing to lose, and nothing you can break. There is no score, no
timer, and no way to do it wrong.

## Starting it

```
cargo run -p mesmerize
```

A window opens and the light begins. Close the window when you are done.

You can resize the window to any shape you like, including full screen. The light fills
whatever shape you give it, and there is always the same amount of it, so a tall window looks
as full as a wide one.

## It breathes

The whole field swells and brightens, then settles and dims, over and over. One full breath
takes eleven seconds, which is about five and a half breaths a minute. The drawing-in is the
shorter half and the letting-go is the longer one.

That pace is not decorative. It is roughly the rhythm that slow-breathing practice aims for,
and it is slow enough to follow comfortably. If you would like to breathe along with it, you
can simply follow the light: breathe in as it brightens and spreads, out as it dims and
gathers.

If you want something more definite to follow, press `b`. A faint ring appears in the middle
that grows as you breathe in and shrinks as you breathe out. It is off to begin with, because
the light already carries the rhythm, and a ring in the middle of the picture is one more
thing to look at rather than something to rest in. Press `b` again to send it away.

The breathing keeps its own time. If you speed the current up or slow it down, the breath
stays exactly where it was.

## Drawing through it

Move the pointer across the window and the current turns around it, as if you had trailed a
finger through water. The wake follows your hand, then loosens and disappears on its own.

Only movement does this. If you rest your hand still, the field is left alone.

## What you can change

Everything below is optional. The piece looks after itself.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `c` | Changes the colours. There are four, and each fades gently into the next. |
| `b` | Shows or hides the breathing ring. |
| `n` | Hold for more light. The longer you hold, the more arrives. |
| `m` | Hold for less. |
| `space` | Stills everything, and again to let it go. |
| `up` / `down` | Hold to make the current faster or slower. |
| `0` | Returns the current to its ordinary pace. |
| `r` | Scatters the light and lets it find its way again. |

The four colour sets are worth trying at different times of day. **Tide** is blues and
sea-greens, and is the one it starts on. **Ember** is plum and coral and warm cream. **Moss**
is greens going to pale gold. **Dusk** is violet going to almost white.

A few reminders sit at the bottom of the window when you begin, along with a count of how
much light there is. They fade away after a few seconds and leave you with just the light.
Press any of the keys above and they come back.

## Things worth trying

Slow it right down. Hold `down` for several seconds and the whole field turns to something
closer to weather than water. Some people find this the best setting to leave running.

Fill it up. Hold `n` for a while. The strands thicken and start to overlap into something more
like cloth than like dust.

Empty it out. Hold `m` instead, and the strands thin until you can follow single points of
light travelling on their own, and see the shape of the current directly.

Still it. Press `space` and the whole thing holds where it is, like a photograph. Press it
again and everything resumes from exactly where it stopped.

Draw a slow circle with the pointer and then take your hand away, and watch how long the
disturbance takes to settle.

## Why the light gathers

The current has no drains in it. Nowhere in the window is a place where the flow ends up, so
the light can never pile into a corner and stay there.

The points of light do gather, though, and that is the interesting part. They are slightly
slow to follow the current, in the way a leaf is slow to follow a stream. That small lateness
is enough to throw them out of the turning centres and collect them along the paths between.
Those gatherings are the bright strands you see. Because the current beneath keeps changing,
the strands are always dissolving and reforming somewhere else, so it never settles into a
finished picture.

The dark, quiet middles are the centres of the turns. The bright ribbons are the paths
between them.
