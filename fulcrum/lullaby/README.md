# Lullaby

Something to fall asleep to.

A sky full of warm light, shaking gently. Over the next twenty-five minutes it cools down. The
shaking gets smaller, each point of light draws in toward a place of its own, and eventually the
whole sky stops moving and sits there, still. Then the light goes out, slowly, until the screen is
black.

The sound carries on after that. It breathes, slower and slower and lower and lower, into a dark
room, and then that stops too and the machine sits quiet.

You do not have to do anything. There is nothing to win, nothing to lose, nothing to keep track
of, and nothing you can do wrong. If you fall asleep in the first two minutes, it will put itself
away without your help.

## Starting it

```
cargo run -p lullaby
```

A window opens and the night begins. Put it somewhere you can half see it, close your eyes, and
leave it alone. Close the window whenever you like.

You can resize the window to any shape, including full screen. The sky stretches to fit and keeps
the same amount of light in it, so a tall window looks as full as a wide one.

## The sound is the important part

You cannot watch a screen with your eyes closed, so a thing you have to look at is a thing you
have to stay awake for. This one is built the other way round.

Every breath it takes, you hear: a warm swell as it draws in, and a longer, quieter hush as it
lets go. As the night wears on both get slower and deeper, and the hush trails off into silence
before the bottom of the out-breath, which leaves you a moment of nothing at the end of every
breath.

The light is spent early on purpose. It is gone by about two thirds of the way through, and the
last third is sound alone in the dark, which is the part that is any use once your eyes have shut.

It starts quiet and takes a minute to come up, so it will not go off in a dark room. Press `s` if
you would rather it did not happen at all.

## It breathes, and the breath gets longer

It starts at ten seconds a breath, which is six a minute. By the end it is sixteen seconds, which
is under four a minute.

The interesting part is where the extra time goes. The in-breath stays where it is, at about four
and a half seconds all night. Everything else is added to the out-breath, which starts at five and
a half seconds and finishes at eleven. That is the shape of it: breathe in the same, breathe out
for longer and longer.

If you would like to breathe along, follow the sound, or watch the large faint glow in the middle
of the window swell and settle. Following it is optional. It works on you a little either way, and
it is easier to fall asleep while ignoring something than while doing something.

## Watching it settle

The sky is not an animation of settling, it is a thing that actually settles.

Each point of light is tied by a very weak spring to a resting place of its own, and is being
shaken at random. At the beginning the shaking wins easily: every light wanders a long way from
its place, they all overlap, and what you see is a haze in constant motion. As the night goes on
the shaking gets weaker and the springs get stronger, so each light's wandering tightens around
its place, and the haze slowly resolves into a sky of separate stars.

The lights are also drawn smaller and sharper as this happens, so the picture comes into focus at
the same time as it comes to rest.

Eventually the shaking stops entirely and every light is sitting exactly on its place, not moving
at all. This happens while there is still a good amount of light left, so you get to see the
finished sky before it goes. It seemed worth arranging that way round: something that stops moving
while you can still see it is restful, and something that goes dark while it is still moving keeps
you looking for it.

There is a soft band of stars running across the sky, thicker than the rest, which is where most
of them end up.

## Nothing in it can happen quickly

However long the night is, and whatever you press, the piece takes at least two and a half minutes
to travel from one end of itself to the other. Shorten the night, restart it, ask for time back:
the plan changes at once and the room follows it at a walk.

So there is no key you can hit, and no accident, that makes the screen brighten or darken suddenly.
That was the first thing designed and everything else was arranged around it.

## If you are still awake

Press `space`. It hands you back four minutes and drifts gently back to where it was a few minutes
ago. You can do that as often as you like.

If you would rather have a longer night to begin with, the number keys set it: `1` is five minutes,
`9` is forty-five, and it starts on `5`, which is twenty-five.

## What you can change

All of it is optional. It looks after itself.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `space` | Still awake. Hands back four minutes. |
| `s` | Sound on or off. Whatever is playing is allowed to finish. |
| `c` | Changes the colours. There are two. |
| `1` to `9` | How long the night is, in five minute steps. |
| `up` / `down` | Hold for a brighter or dimmer room. |
| `r` | Begins again, with a different sky. |

A few reminders sit at the bottom of the window when you start, along with how long there is to
go. They fade away after a few seconds. Press anything and they come back, and once the sky is out
they cannot come back at all, because there is no version of this where words appear on a dark
screen at two in the morning.

The colours are both warm. **Ember** is rust through to a pale cream, and is the one it starts on.
**Moonlight** is a paler, drier set, closer to bone. Neither has any real blue in it, which is the
one piece of received wisdom about light and sleep that seemed worth building around, and it also
happens to be the right choice for a picture whose whole life is spent getting dimmer: a dim warm
colour still reads as light, and a dim blue one just reads as cold.

## Things worth trying

Set a long night with `9` and leave it running while you read. The first ten minutes of it are
barely doing anything, which is the point.

Watch one single light, near the beginning, and keep watching that one. You will see it wander a
long way and then wander less, and less, and then stop. Every other light in the window is doing
the same thing on its own schedule.

Press `r` when it has gone dark. It comes back up over the following couple of minutes with a
completely different sky in it.

Turn the sound off with `s` and watch it in silence once, just to see the settling. Then turn the
sound on and do not watch it at all, which is what it is actually for.

## What this is not

It is a quiet room with a slow breath in it. It is not a treatment for anything, it does not know
whether you are asleep, and it does not measure anything about you or keep any record of the night.

Slow breathing with a long out-breath, dim warm light, and less and less to look at are all
reasonable things to have around at bedtime, and this is those three things arranged carefully.
That is the whole claim.
