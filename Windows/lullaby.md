# lullaby

A field of light that cools, settles, dims to black, and goes on breathing after you close your
eyes.

## The idea

A sky full of warm light, shaking gently. Over the next twenty-five minutes it cools down. The
shaking gets smaller, each point of light draws in toward a place of its own, and eventually the
whole sky stops moving and sits there, still. Then the light goes out, slowly, until the screen
is black. The sound carries on after that, breathing slower and lower into a dark room, and then
that stops too.

The settling is real, not an animation of settling. Each point of light is tied by a very weak
spring to a resting place of its own, and is being shaken at random. At the beginning the
shaking wins easily and the sky is a haze in constant motion; as the night goes on the shaking
weakens and the springs strengthen, so the haze resolves into separate stars.

The sound is the important part, because you cannot watch a screen with your eyes closed. Every
breath is a warm swell drawing in and a longer, quieter hush letting go. The in-breath stays at
about four and a half seconds all night, and all the extra time goes to the out-breath, which
starts at five and a half seconds and finishes at eleven. The light is spent early on purpose,
so the last third is sound alone in the dark.

Nothing in it can happen quickly. Whatever you press, it takes at least two and a half minutes
to travel from one end of itself to the other, so there is no key you can hit that makes the
screen brighten or darken suddenly.

## Working it

You do not have to do anything. There is nothing to win, nothing to lose, and nothing you can do
wrong; it puts itself away without your help.

| | |
|---|---|
| `space` | still awake: hands back four minutes |
| `s` | sound on or off |
| `c` | changes the colours (Ember and Moonlight, both warm) |
| `1` to `9` | how long the night is, in five minute steps; it starts on `5`, which is twenty-five |
| `up` / `down` | hold for a brighter or dimmer room |
| `r` | begin again, with a different sky |

It starts quiet and takes a minute to come up, so it will not go off in a dark room.
